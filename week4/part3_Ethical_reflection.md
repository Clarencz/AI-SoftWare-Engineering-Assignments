Part 3: Ethical Reflection (10%)Prompt: Your predictive model from Task 3 is deployed in a company. Discuss potential biases in the dataset and how fairness tools like IBM AI Fairness 360 could address them.Even if the model from Task 3 (predicting "priority" from "features") is highly accurate, it is dangerously susceptible to encoding and amplifying human biases, leading to unfair resource allocation.1. Potential Biases in the Dataset:The dataset of "issue priorities" is not an objective ground truth; it is a collection of subjective decisions made by past developers or managers.Reporter Bias: Issues reported by senior or more "vocal" engineers may be historically labeled "High" priority, while similar or even more severe issues reported by junior or remote engineers are labeled "Low."Team/Module Bias: The prompt mentioned "underrepresented teams." Bugs from "prestige" teams (e.g., core backend, new features) may be prioritized over bugs from "maintenance" teams (e.g., documentation, UI/UX, internal tools), even if the UI bugs have a greater impact on the user.Quantification Bias: It is easier to measure the impact of a backend crash (e.g., 500 error) than a UI/UX "friction" bug (e.g., "checkout flow is confusing"). The dataset will be biased towards prioritizing easily quantifiable bugs, starving user-facing teams of resources.The model will learn these biases. It might learn, for example, reporter_seniority < 2 -> Low_Priority, effectively silencing junior developers.2. How IBM AI Fairness 360 (AIF360) Could Address This:AIF360 provides a toolkit to detect and mitigate these biases.Detect: First, we would define "protected attributes," such as reporter_team or reporter_seniority. We would then use AIF360 metrics like Statistical Parity Difference (which checks if all teams get "High" priority at roughly the same rate) and Equal Opportunity Difference (which checks if actual high-priority bugs are identified equally for all teams). These metrics would provide a quantitative "proof" of the bias.Mitigate: Once bias is confirmed, AIF360 offers algorithms to fix it:Pre-processing (Reweighing): The "Reweighing" algorithm can be used to give a higher weight to training samples from the underrepresented team (e.g., the UI/UX team). This forces the model to pay more attention to their issues and learn their patterns, balancing the dataset without altering the data itself.In-processing (Adversarial Debiasing): We could retrain the model using an algorithm that simultaneously tries to (A) predict the priority and (B) not predict the team. This "adversary" forces the model to learn signals of priority that are independent of the team that reported it.
